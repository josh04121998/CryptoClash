- **Subject/background sharing one hue.** Frogs are green-on-green, Builders blue-on-blue, Degens red-on-black. Moon Dog works partly by accident: a warm gold dog against a cool blue-black field is complementary contrast, so the subject separates. **Fixed in the two shared style blocks** (so it applies to all 78 prompts at once): an explicit medium-shot instruction, an explicit "brighter than the backdrop, never a murky near-black one" exposure instruction, and a lighting formula that moves the faction hue to the **rim light and background glow** while putting a **warm neutral key on the subject**. Also aligned the stated ratio to "roughly 2:3", matching what Grok actually produces and what the § v2.0 frame lesson already settled ("Grok doesn't honor [exact pixels]; generate close to 2:3, resize in code") — the blocks had been asking for 4:5 that was never going to arrive.

Grey now belongs to their clothes, warm ordinary office light to their faces, cool neon chaos to the background — which also finally gives Normies the warm-on-cool separation that makes the Doggos read. ## How to use this document

1. Paste a card's **Prompt** block into Grok exactly as written. Don't paraphrase it — the wording is deliberate (see the negative constraints below).
2. Before accepting a result, check it against `branding.md` §9.1's rules: no text/numbers/logos/watermarks/card-border baked in, reads as one clear silhouette even shrunk small, no rarity-specific glow/frame in the art itself.
3. Save the accepted image as `client/src/assets/cards/{id}.jpg` (the id is given after each card name below — this is `cardArt.ts`'s lookup key, same convention Moon Dog already uses).
4. Tick it off the checklist at the end of this doc.
5. **Generate every card's Standard illustration first, in full, before touching Full Art/Ultra/Secret Edition art or the frame templates** — those are lower-priority, later-stage work (`collectibility.md` §8/§10, `branding.md` §9.3).

Nothing here needs Foil or Condition/Floor Grade art — both are pure CSS effects applied at render time over the Standard illustration (`collectibility.md` §5/§7). Never generate a "foil version" or a "graded version" of an image.

## The two reusable style blocks

Every character-card prompt below already has the **Character Suffix** folded in at the end — you don't need to append anything extra. It's shown here once just so you know what's constant across every card and why:

> **Character Suffix:** Semi-realistic illustrated character art, anime-adjacent rendering with real lighting and shading — not flat vector, not photorealistic. Medium shot: the subject's head and upper body fill most of the frame, close to the camera — not a small full-body figure standing in a wide landscape. Bright, confident key light on the subject, clearly brighter than the backdrop behind it — a rich, deep-contrast scene, never a murky near-black one. Dark trading-floor backdrop kept simple and low behind the subject: a lit NYC skyline low in the frame, or a neon monitor-grid floor, thin scanline texture, soft neon glow. Portrait orientation, roughly 2:3 aspect ratio, subject centered, one clear readable silhouette that still reads at thumbnail size. No text, no numbers, no logos, no watermark, no signature, no card border or frame, no rarity glow baked in, no readable words anywhere in the scene.

Neutral Items/Spells/Secrets are objects, not characters (`branding.md` §9.4), so they use a slightly different suffix, also already folded into their prompts below:

> **Object Suffix:** Semi-realistic illustrated object/artifact art, same rendering register as the character cards — anime-adjacent, real lighting and shading. The object fills most of the frame, close to the camera, lit brightly against the backdrop — a rich, deep-contrast scene, never a murky near-black one. Dark trading-floor backdrop kept simple behind it, thin scanline texture, soft neon glow. Portrait orientation, roughly 2:3 aspect ratio, subject centered, clean readable shape even at thumbnail size. No text, no numbers, no logos, no watermark, no signature, no card border or frame, no readable words anywhere in the scene.

Faction accent colors (sampled into each prompt, from `branding.md` §2): Doggos `#f5a623` · Frogs `#4ade80` · Degens `#ef4444` · Crypto Bros `#facc15` · Builders `#60a5fa` · Normies `#a3a3a3`. Neutral cards use the core brand pair instead (gold `#f2b705` / terminal green `#00e28a`), not a faction color.

## Frogs (accent `#4ade80`) — chaotic, mischievous, degenerate energy; frogs/toads in trading-floor attire, sometimes glitch-warped

**v2 fix (2026-09-17):** the first-pass generations (`branding/assets/Cards/Frogs/`) all came back with two real, consistent problems, found by actually looking at the rendered files rather than assuming the prompts were fine because Doggos' worked:
1. **No trading-floor personification.** Every other faction's prompts put a worn or held prop on the subject (Doggos' blazers/ties, Builders' hoodies/tool belts) — the Frogs prompts never did, so Grok defaulted to literal wildlife-documentary photos of real frogs/toads with no costuming at all, reading as out of place next to every other faction's cast of characters.
2. **Monitor/screen backgrounds rendered as blurry-but-legible pseudo-text** (readable-looking glowing terminal lines, not abstract glow) — a direct Character Suffix violation ("no readable words anywhere in the scene") that Doggos' prompts never hit because none of them put a screen with visible "lines" in frame the same way.
3. A third, narrower issue on **Frog Swarm** specifically: tiny subjects scattered thin across the whole frame don't survive "reads as one clear silhouette shrunk small" — needs a tight, cohesive cluster like Puppy Swarm's "tumbling pile," not a diffuse scatter.

## Rarity frame templates (`branding.md` §9.6) — do these last, after all 73 illustrations exist

One reusable frame/border shell per rarity, no card-specific content — the second rendering pipeline (`tools/card-render/`) composites these behind a card's real art + data-driven text.

**v2.0 — minimalist redesign (2026-09-14).** Replaces everything below with a completely different, much simpler direction. The v1.x ornate-fantasy-metal design (bat-wing corners, faceted gem sockets, four carved regions) is dropped entirely in favor of a clean, modern, Pokémon-style border, decided after comparing accepted candidates directly. Why the switch, and what carries over as settled fact for this new design:

- **Two boxes only, not four.** No separate name-plate band, no separate footer strip, no gem sockets baked into the frame at all. Cost/attack/health badges and the name are already drawn by the compositor as CSS shapes (`tools/card-render/template.html`, proven working on the shipped Moon Dog render) sitting on *top* of this frame image — the frame itself only needs to provide (1) an art box and (2) a description box, stacked with nothing else.
- **Card fills the canvas edge to edge.** The accepted reference (`branding/assets/Rarity/grok-image-12c745e5-...jpg`) has only ~2–3% margin around the card and a clearly visible ~3%-width border — not a small card floating on a padded canvas (a rejected candidate had ~10% dead margin on each side and a border so thin it nearly disappeared).
- **Box proportions: ~56% art, ~36% description**, with the remaining ~8% as top/bottom border margin. Measured directly off the accepted reference and cross-checked against a real product-photo mockup the box sizing was compared to — both land within a point of each other, so this split is confirmed right, not a guess.
- **Interior fill color no longer matters and is not specified.** Earlier versions of this doc demanded an exact `#0b120e` match for chroma-keying purposes. That was solving a problem the current compositor design doesn't have: both boxes get fully painted over by code (art image in box 1, a drawn panel + text in box 2), so whatever flat color Grok renders is invisible in the final card either way. Plain black (what the accepted reference happens to use) is fine.
- **No ornamentation.** No corner brackets, no gem sockets, no icons — a single border stroke, rounded corners, nothing else besides the material treatment below. This is a deliberate simplification, not a placeholder; don't add detail back in without discussing it first.
- **No exact-pixel demand** (carried over from the v1.x lesson — Grok doesn't honor it). Generate at a portrait ratio close to 2:3; resize to the compositor's target size in code.

**v2.1 — real metal material, not a flat color fill (2026-09-14).** A flat solid-color border (literally what v2.0 asked for) read as cheap/boring once generated. Fixed by asking for the border to be a genuine polished metal material — brushed texture, a directional specular highlight, real light-to-dark tonal range — instead of one uniform hex value. Confirmed by comparison: a flat `#22c55e` fill looked flat; a version with real gradient (sampled off the accepted Uncommon frame, ranging from a deep shadowed `(23,93,59)` up to a bright `(113,196,152)` highlight) looked noticeably more premium and is the one that got picked. This is a material/rendering change only — the two-box structure, proportions, and no-ornamentation rule above are unchanged.
