# 0. Project Direction (read this first)

The end goal, per the user (2026-09-04): **bring the Pokémon-collectibles experience on-chain** — collecting/chasing rarity is the *main* draw, not a side layer bolted onto a card game. Battle gameplay (engine, factions, matches) is the vehicle; the thing players are meant to get hooked on is owning, showing off, and trading cards.

Go-to-market is crypto-native: launch via a token, acquire early users from the crypto/Web3 community rather than a broad mainstream audience first. Consequence: **wallet-connect is the primary identity system from day one** (not email/social-first-then-wallet-later, which is what `architecture.md` Section 9 originally recommended — see Section 5 below for that reconciliation). Guiding principle from the user: *least possible friction to just play* — no login wall in front of Play vs AI or Play Online; a wallet is only asked for the moment something needs to persist (a saved deck, eventually a collection/NFT).

Explicit non-goal: literal Hearthstone production values (painted art, voice, orchestral audio, live-ops team) — not achievable solo/small-team and not the point. The bar is "complete, fun, well-balanced, genuinely collectible," not "AAA."

**Open, unresolved as of session 12 (2026-09-08): the user is reconsidering the game's actual name**, "Crypto Clash" — raised in passing during a branding/asset session, not yet discussed in depth. Until this is settled: don't bake the current name into anything that would be expensive to redo (character art, a finalized logo lockup) — see `branding.md` Section 1.

**Token/economy direction, settled 2026-09-07 (not yet built — design target for spec.md/architecture.md, see those files for the full reasoning):** Coins stay exactly as designed (free, earn-only, gates the core loop) — real-money/stablecoin pack purchases become a *separate* cash-shop path alongside them (spec.md Section 21), the industry-normal "whales buy more shots at rares" lever, deliberately never routed through the token so pack pricing isn't exposed to its volatility. The external token is a marketing/community/staking layer riding this cycle's memefi wave, thematically anchored to the Doggos faction (dogs being the proven meme-coin lineage) — any staking rewards or buybacks must be funded from real revenue (a cut of cash-shop pack sales + marketplace fees, spec.md Section 27's new Funding Rule), never from token emissions, specifically to avoid the Axie Infinity SLP collapse pattern (emission-funded rewards only hold up while new-buyer inflow outpaces emission — structurally unsustainable once growth slows). Leaning toward self-listing the token (own contract, own seeded liquidity pool, e.g. Uniswap/Aerodrome on Base) over a pump.fun-style launchpad, conditional on pairing it with real distribution (the game + Doggos meme identity + an airdrop to early players, not just hoping a DEX listing draws attention on its own) and visibly locking liquidity to preempt rug suspicion — see architecture.md Section 8's new subsection. Exact tokenomics (supply, allocation, launch timing) still open.

**Chain choice, discussed 2026-09-08 (session 9) — leaning Robinhood Chain, not locked in:** the user asked for a pros/cons comparison against Solana. Robinhood Chain is an Ethereum-compatible Arbitrum L2 (mainnet launched 2026-07-01, settles to Ethereum, OpenSea/Uniswap/Chainlink integrated day one) — it inherits the exact "EVM L2, mature tooling" reasoning `architecture.md` Section 12 already used to favor Base/Polygon over alternatives, at essentially zero extra engineering cost, since the whole wallet-connect stack (`ethers`/`siwe`/EIP-1193/`personal_sign`) already speaks EVM. Solana would mean rebuilding that already-shipped, already-tested auth layer from scratch (different wallet model, ed25519 signing, no SIWE equivalent) — a real cost, not a preference, that tipped the recommendation. Real risk acknowledged, not dismissed: Robinhood Chain had only ~2 months of mainnet history at the time of this decision, so its early NFT-volume numbers (~$1.05M/day) may be launch-week energy rather than durable traction; Solana's NFT ecosystem, despite the user's (well-founded, current) scam-reputation concern, is far more battle-tested. **Still not built** — `architecture.md` Section 12's own guidance (don't build the on-chain layer before the game/economy is proven) still applies; this is a direction, not a commitment, and nothing stops building the Web3 service chain-agnostically per the architecture doc's existing recommendation and deciding for real at deploy time.

# 2. Battle Engine (`engine/`)

Implements batlleSpec.md's full "First Prototype" checklist (Section 32), **Items included**:

- Turn loop: Energy (1→10, refills each turn), Draw, Play, Attack, End
- 5-slot board with adjacency (Section 5/8) — auras key off it (Moon Dog's "+1 Attack while next to another Doggo")
- Creatures, Spells, **and Items** (equip-style buffs/keyword grants — see below)
- All five launch keywords (Section 11): **Rush**, **Guard**, **Stealth**, **Burn**, **HODL**
- **Volatility & all five Market Events** (Sections 16-18): MARKET_CRASH, PUMP, LIQUIDATION, FOMO, BLACK_SWAN
- Fatigue (escalating damage on empty-deck draw), hand size cap
- Server-authoritative by construction: the engine is pure/deterministic (`seed` + ordered `Intent`s → identical result every time), so replay/anti-cheat (Section 31) falls out for free — `state.log` already is that replay

**Card pool:** 60 templates. All six spec.md Section 6 factions now have real depth and their own standalone 30-card deck (`cards.ts`, indexed by `DECKS`/`getDeck()`):

| Faction | Deck export | Signature mechanic |
|---|---|---|
| Doggos | `SAMPLE_DECK` | Swarm/adjacency (existing) |
| Frogs | `FROG_SAMPLE_DECK` | `copyRandomFriendly` — copy a random friendly creature |
| Builders | `BUILDER_SAMPLE_DECK` | `draw` — card advantage/combo |
| Degens | `DEGEN_SAMPLE_DECK` | `damage` targeted at `selfPlayer` — pay your own HP for power |
| Crypto Bros | `CRYPTOBRO_SAMPLE_DECK` | `gainEnergy` (this-turn burst) / `gainMaxEnergy` (permanent ramp) |
| Normies | `NORMIE_SAMPLE_DECK` | `heal` — steady, defensive toolkit |

Plus **5 Neutral Items** (`buffTarget` for permanent +stat upgrades, `grantKeywordTarget` for temporary Rush/Guard grants — Guard/Rush are the only keywords `combat.ts` checks generically via `keywords ∪ tempKeywords`, so those are the only two safe to grant this way).

**Session 4:** every non-token template carries a `rarity` (spec.md Section 13's tiers — Common through Genesis), assigned by a simple cost-based heuristic (cost 0-1→Common, 2→Uncommon, 3→Rare, 4→Epic, 5→Legendary; no current template uses Mythic/Genesis, reserved for scarcer future content). It's gameplay-adjacent metadata only — never read by combat/effect resolution, purely there for pack odds and collection-screen grouping. The heuristic is a placeholder, not tuned game/economy design.

**New this session (session 5) — a real bot opponent (`bot.ts`):** `takeBotTurn` was a pure greedy heuristic (play whatever's affordable into the first empty slot, always attack face unless a Guard forces otherwise) — now it actually reasons about combat and positioning, while staying the same fast/synchronous/zero-extra-randomness function the engine's determinism guarantee depends on. It computes real lethal (sum of `getEffectiveAttack` across everything still eligible to attack ≥ enemy HP) and only goes face-first when that's true; otherwise it looks for a "clean kill" — an enemy creature it can kill without dying itself, preferring the highest-effective-attack option when more than one qualifies — and only falls back to face damage when no good trade exists. Creature placement now prefers an empty slot adjacent to an existing friendly creature when that would actually trigger that neighbor's `adjacentSameFaction` aura (e.g. landing next to Moon Dog), instead of always taking the first empty slot. Hand-play order changed too: cards are now tried biggest-cost-first each pass rather than hand-index order, so a big card doesn't get stranded behind a small one that happened to be drawn first. 3 new tests in `engine/test/engine.test.ts` (clean-kill-over-face, still-face-when-lethal, aura-aware placement) plus the existing 42 — 45/45 passing. Sanity-simulated 200 bot-vs-bot games across all 6 faction decks and mixed seeds with no exceptions.

See `card-schema.md` for the full effect DSL (now 10 `EffectAction` kinds), and `engine/README.md` for how to run/extend it.

# 3. Client (`client/`)

Two play modes, both live, both fronted by a **deck picker** (`DeckPicker.tsx`) — pick a starter deck, or (if wallet-connected) one of your own saved decks, before a match starts:

- **Play vs AI** — runs the engine directly in the browser against a greedy-heuristic bot (`takeBotTurn`), which now also picks a random deck each match for variety. No backend, no wallet required. This is the permanent practice/tutorial mode, not a placeholder.
- **Play Online** — real matchmaking through the Railway match server; the resolved 30-card list (starter or custom) rides along on the `findMatch` message — the server re-validates it (`validateDeck`) rather than trusting the client, falling back to the default deck if it's missing or illegal.

Board/hand/interaction UI (`MatchView`) is shared between both modes, parameterized by which player is "me," so every fix or feature (the Volatility meter, keyword badges, etc.) applies to both at once. Targeting distinguishes friendly-target cards (Items — click your own board) from enemy-target cards (damage/burn spells — click the opponent's board/portrait), via the shared `targetsFriendlyCreature()` helper (also used by the bot, so it doesn't waste Items targeting the wrong side).

**Wallet-connect + deck builder** (`useWallet.ts`, `DeckBuilder.tsx`, `MyDecksScreen.tsx`; session 3):

Saved decks are real Postgres rows, scoped per-wallet, and appear back in the match deck-picker.
- Bundle-size cost: `siwe`/`ethers` (needed for SIWE message construction/signing) added ~670KB gzipped-~95KB to the client bundle, and required a Node `Buffer` polyfill (`vite-plugin-node-polyfills`, scoped to just `buffer`+`process` — deliberately *not* `crypto`, which would've pulled in a vulnerable `elliptic` transitive dependency for a polyfill nothing here needs). Worth revisiting in a later polish pass if load time becomes a concern.

Fixed by normalizing with `ethers`'s `getAddress()` right after reading the account. `ethers` was already an implicit, hoisted dependency (a `siwe` peer dependency, already counted in the bundle-size note above) — made explicit in `client/package.json` rather than relying on hoisting from a sibling workspace's devDependency. Side effect: the client bundle actually *shrank* (894KB→776KB minified) once Vite could resolve/tree-shake `ethers` from one clean explicit dependency instead of whatever implicit path it was finding before. Verified with two scripted-browser checks: the exact reported address no longer throws (fails later, as expected, only because the mock signature isn't real), and a full real-wallet sign-in with a deliberately lowercased address succeeds end-to-end.

**New this session (session 7) — wallet-switching (`useWallet.ts`):** raised by the user mid-session as a real gap — `connect()`'s `eth_requestAccounts` won't show MetaMask's account picker once a site is already authorized, and there was no listener for the active account changing outside the app. Fixed two ways: (1) a `switchWallet()` action, wired to a renamed "Switch Wallet" button (previously the connected-address button doubled as Disconnect — now split into two: clicking the address forces `wallet_requestPermissions` to reopen the picker, then re-runs the normal SIWE connect flow for whichever account is chosen; a separate "Disconnect" button does what the old combined button did); (2) an `accountsChanged` event listener — since the session token is SIWE-signed for one specific address, a mid-session account switch inside the wallet extension now clears the stale session and prompts "click Connect Wallet to sign in with it" rather than silently continuing to act as the old account. Deliberately doesn't auto-re-sign on `accountsChanged` — popping an unsolicited signature prompt the instant someone switches accounts for an unrelated reason would be surprising.

**New this session (session 10) — multi-wallet picker (`useWallet.ts`, `WalletPicker.tsx`):** session 7's wallet-switching fix turned out to be half the story — it could re-prompt for a different *account* within whichever extension `window.ethereum` happened to point at, but with more than one wallet extension installed (MetaMask + Coinbase Wallet + Rabby, etc.) there was never a way to pick *which extension* at all, since `window.ethereum` is just whichever one claimed that global first. Fixed with [EIP-6963](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-6963) "Multi Injected Provider Discovery": the app now listens for `eip6963:announceProvider` events (and fires `eip6963:requestProvider` on mount to catch wallets that already announced before the listener attached) to enumerate every installed wallet, tracked in `useWallet.ts`'s new `discoveredWallets`. `connect()`/`switchWallet()` are unchanged and stay zero-friction when there's 0 or 1 wallet installed (the common case); `App.tsx` only shows the new `WalletPicker.tsx` overlay when `discoveredWallets.length > 1`, letting the user pick an extension by name/icon before signing in. `useWallet.ts` now tracks which specific provider actually signed the session (`activeProvider`) rather than always assuming `window.ethereum`, so the `accountsChanged` listener and future switches target the right extension. Verified with a scripted-browser (Playwright) test simulating two independently-announcing EIP-6963 wallets: confirmed the picker lists both by name, connecting via one produces that wallet's real address, and clicking "Switch wallet" with two installed re-opens the picker (rather than silently reusing the same extension) and correctly switches identity when the other is chosen.

**New this session (session 7, second wave) — Leaderboard (`LeaderboardScreen.tsx`, `server/src/leaderboardRepo.ts`):** raised directly by the user ("people love that in games — most wins, highest win-rate, most Coins earned"). Three tabs — Most Wins, Win Rate, Coins Earned — each a top-20 board plus a "you're ranked #N" line for whoever's connected. Deliberately **no new schema or write path**: every number is a read-side aggregate `group by`/`rank() over (...)` query straight off the existing `coin_transactions` audit log (session 5's `match_win`/`match_loss`/`match_draw` reasons for the first two, every positive credit — match rewards, welcome bonus, daily rewards, quests — for "Coins Earned"), so it can never drift out of sync with the ledger it's reading and needed zero migration. "Coins Earned" is lifetime gross earned, not current balance — spending on packs/crafting doesn't drop your rank, which matters since engaged players spend the most. Win Rate gates entry at `MIN_GAMES_FOR_WIN_RATE` (5, first-pass number) so a 1-0 record can't top a 40-10 veteran; below the threshold, `getMyWinRate` still reports real games/wins with `rank: null` so the UI can show "play N more matches to qualify" instead of just hiding the player. The board itself is public — reachable from the main menu with no wallet at all (same "no login wall to look" principle as Packs' `GET /api/packs`) — only the "you're ranked #N" line needs a token, and that's soft: `GET /api/leaderboard/*` never 401s, an absent/invalid token just omits `mine`. # 4. Accounts, Decks & Collection Backend (`server/`)

Started session 3 (accounts + decks) and extended this session (session 4) with `architecture.md` Section 6's rarity/collectible layer — editions + instances, no `card_templates` table (see Section 6's deviation note in `architecture.md`, same reasoning as `decks.cards`: gameplay identity stays in `CARD_POOL`, nothing duplicates it into Postgres). Lives in the same Railway service as the match server (same "Game Backend" box the architecture doc's own diagram already draws), exposed as `/api/*` REST routes alongside the existing WebSocket endpoint.

- **Schema**, applied via `npm run migrate --workspace=server` (a small hand-rolled runner — no ORM/framework):
  - `0001_accounts_and_decks.sql`: `accounts` (wallet address, lowercased, unique) and `decks` (account-scoped, `cards` as a JSON array, cascade-deletes with the account).
  - `0002_editions_and_instances.sql`: `card_editions` (`template_id` text — a `CARD_POOL` key, not an FK — `edition_type` standard/first_edition/legendary/genesis, unique per template+type) and `card_instances` (`owner_id` → `accounts`, `edition_id` → `card_editions`, both cascade-delete, plus nullable `serial_number`/`onchain_token_id` and an `is_foil` flag for when packs/minting actually populate them).
  - `0003_coins_and_packs.sql` (new this session): `accounts.coins_balance` (a cached running total), `coin_transactions` (append-only audit log — positive amount = earn, negative = spend, both cascade-delete with the account), `pack_openings` (one row per pack pulled: `pack_type`, `coins_spent`, `cards` (the template ids pulled, in reveal order), and the `mulberry32` `seed` the roll used — reproducible and auditable, same determinism-by-construction story as match replays).
- **Auth** (`server/src/auth.ts`): Sign-In with Ethereum — nonce issue/consume (one-time, 5-minute TTL, in-memory), SIWE message verification (`siwe` package) checked against a configured domain (replay/cross-app-reuse protection), session as a signed JWT (`jose`). Fully tested with real ECDSA signing via `ethers.Wallet` — no mocking of the crypto itself. `findOrCreateAccount` (`accounts.ts`) now also returns `isNew` (via Postgres's `xmax = 0` upsert trick) — the welcome-bonus grant's one-time-only gate, see Coins below.
- **Collection** (`server/src/collectionRepo.ts`): `grantStartingCollection` gives every account `MAX_COPIES_PER_CARD` (3) standard-edition instances of every **Common**-rarity template (narrowed this session from "every non-token template" — see Coins/Packs below for why) — called on every sign-in, idempotent (tops up rather than duplicates, so it also back-fills any Common template added to the pool after an account's first sign-in). `getCollectionCounts` aggregates owned copies per template id; `validateOwnership` checks a prospective deck's card counts against them. New this session: `grantCardInstances` — adds exact template ids as new instances (duplicates included, no top-up capping), the shared primitive packs use to actually grant a pull; takes a `PoolClient` so it composes into packsRepo's own transaction.
- **Coins & Packs** (`server/src/coinsRepo.ts`, `server/src/packsRepo.ts`): `grantWelcomeBonus` credits a new account (gated on `isNew`, not idempotency-checked separately) a flat `WELCOME_BONUS_COINS` (3,000 — a placeholder number, not tuned; there's still no real Coins *earn* loop, since matches aren't tied to authenticated accounts yet). `openPack` is one atomic transaction: locks the account's balance row (`for update`), checks affordability, rolls `PACK_DEFINITIONS.standard`'s 5 cards against a fresh seed via the engine's own `mulberry32` (exported from `engine/src/index.ts`), debits Coins, grants the instances, and logs the pull — a mid-way failure can never charge without granting or vice versa. Rolling (`rollPackCards`) is a pure function of `(packType, seed)`, tested independently of the DB/coin side.
  - **Session 6 — real odds design, replacing the "everything's basically Common" placeholder:** `NORMAL_ODDS` (Common 65% / Uncommon 25% / Rare 8% / Epic 1.8% / Legendary 0.2%) for the first 4 slots, `LAST_SLOT_ODDS` (Uncommon 52% / Rare 33% / Epic 12% / Legendary 3%) for the 5th so every pack still guarantees at least one Uncommon+. Against today's 63-template pool this gives ~0.038 expected Legendary pulls/pack (~1 every 26 packs) and ~0.23 expected Epic+/pack — a real long-tail chase curve, though still a first pass to revisit against actual telemetry, not final tuned numbers. A new `PACK_ELIGIBLE_RARITIES` set (Common–Legendary only) is checked at module load against both odds tables — Mythic and Genesis throw if anyone ever adds a weight for them here, enforcing spec.md Section 16's "permanently capped" promise for Genesis in code, not just in a comment.
  - **Session 6 — foils, a second/independent collectible axis:** every pack card also rolls a flat, rarity-independent `FOIL_CHANCE` (8%) — any template, Common through Legendary, can come out foil, closer to a Pokémon "shiny" than a value multiplier stacked on rarity. Stored on `card_instances.is_foil` (the migration 0002 column existed but was unused until now). `grantCardInstances` (`collectionRepo.ts`) now takes `PackCard[]` (`{templateId, isFoil}`) instead of bare template ids, inserting `is_foil` per instance via two zipped `unnest()` calls in one `INSERT ... SELECT` (lockstep since Postgres 10 — not a cross join). `getCollectionSummary` returns owned counts *and* foil counts together in one query (a conditional aggregate — `count(...) filter (where is_foil)` — caught and fixed in this session's code-review pass, which had originally split this into two separate round-trip queries). `GET /api/collection` now returns `{ owned, foils }` (additive — existing `owned`-only consumers are unaffected). First Edition and Genesis/Mythic are deliberately *not* pack outcomes at all (see the `PACK_ELIGIBLE_RARITIES` note above and spec.md Section 17) — both need their own grant mechanism (a time-boxed print-run flag; an event/achievement path) that doesn't exist yet.
- **Crafting** (`server/src/craftingRepo.ts`, new this session): a third ledger alongside Coins — `accounts.dust_balance` + append-only `dust_transactions` (`0004_dust_and_crafting.sql`), fully separate from Coins, never interchangeable. `disenchantCards` converts `count` owned copies of a template into Dust (non-foil instances consumed first via `order by is_foil asc`, protecting a player's foil pulls from a bulk disenchant by default); `craftCard` spends Dust to grant one new standard, non-foil instance via the existing `grantCardInstances`. Both lock the `accounts` row first (`select ... for update`) before touching `card_instances`, serializing concurrent disenchant/craft calls on the same account the same way `openPack` serializes concurrent pack opens. `CRAFT_ELIGIBLE_RARITIES` (Uncommon–Legendary) excludes Common — allowing it would let a player farm Dust for free, since `grantStartingCollection` re-tops every account's Commons on every sign-in — and excludes Mythic/Genesis for the same permanently-capped-supply reason packs do. Dust values (Uncommon 10/70, Rare 20/100, Epic 100/400, Legendary 400/1600 — disenchant/craft) are anchored to Hearthstone's long-tested disenchant economy, a first design pass per spec.md Section 18. `getCraftRates()` is pure/no-DB, served over `GET /api/craft/rates` so the client never hardcodes a second copy of these numbers.
- **API** (`server/src/httpApi.ts`): `GET/POST /api/auth/nonce|verify`, `GET /api/collection`, `GET/POST /api/decks`, `PUT/DELETE /api/decks/:id`, `GET /api/coins`, `GET /api/packs` (public), `POST /api/packs/open` (`402` if under-funded, `400` for an unknown pack type), plus new this session `GET /api/craft/rates` (public), `GET /api/dust` (authenticated), `POST /api/craft/disenchant` (`409` if under-owned), `POST /api/craft/craft` (`402` if under-funded, `400` for an uncraftable template). Every deck write re-validates against both `validateDeck()` (pool-legality) and `validateOwnership()` server-side, `422` on either failing. Cross-account deck access returns `404` (not `403`), so existence isn't leaked.
- **Tests**: `auth.test.ts` (pure, no DB). `db.test.ts` + `api.test.ts` (real Postgres integration) — coins (welcome-bonus grant/idempotency-via-`isNew`/per-account scoping) and packs (deterministic rolling, a full open debiting Coins and granting instances, insufficient-Coins rejection charging/granting nothing, `grantCardInstances` preserving duplicates past the 3-copy cap) coverage, plus the existing deck-CRUD fixtures reworked to an all-Common 30-card deck now that the starting grant no longer covers Rare+. Both still `describe.skip` without `DATABASE_URL`.
  - **Session 6 additions:** a 500-seed sweep asserting `rollPackCards` never produces a Mythic/Genesis card (the hard exclusion, not just an unlikely outcome to not hit), a 500-seed sweep asserting the observed foil rate lands strictly between 0% and 20% (a real, nonzero-but-minority rate, not a tight statistical bound), `grantCardInstances`/`getCollectionSummary` coverage confirming `is_foil` survives per-instance through a duplicate grant, and a full crafting suite (disenchant crediting the right Dust and removing instances, foil-protection ordering, insufficient-copies/-Dust rejections charging/granting nothing, the Common/token/unknown-template guard rail, and an HTTP-level round trip disenchanting then crafting a different card). Ran and passed in full (58/58) against a real local Postgres (Docker `postgres:16-alpine`, migrations applied fresh) this session, plus full scripted-browser (Playwright) runs through wallet-connect → pack-open → foil reveal → Collection screen, and separately through granting duplicates → Crafting screen → disenchant → craft → Collection screen, per Section 3.

**Session 7 — Daily rewards + Quests (`dailyRepo.ts`, `questsRepo.ts`, migration `0005`):** the second half of the Coins-earn loop (STATUS.md roadmap step 10; spec.md Section 21). Both a fourth Coins source (via `applyCoinDeltaOnClient`, same ledger primitive as everything else) and a reason to come back and play *today*, on top of the one-time welcome bonus and match results.
- **Daily login** (`dailyRepo.ts`): a 7-day escalating reward table (`DAILY_REWARDS`, 50→60→75→90→110→140→250), keyed by `accounts.last_daily_claim_day`/`accounts.daily_streak`. Claiming locks the account row, checks it hasn't already claimed *today* (UTC), then either extends yesterday's streak by one or resets to 1 if a day was missed — the streak isn't capped at 7, it cycles (day 8 pays day 1's rate again), so a long streak keeps paying rather than plateauing. Both "today" and the stored claim day are plain `YYYY-MM-DD` UTC strings (a `text` column, not Postgres `date`) — `pg` parses `date` into a JS `Date` object by default, which would reintroduce exactly the local/UTC ambiguity this is trying to avoid.
- **Quests** (`questsRepo.ts`): a fixed, code-defined list (`QUEST_DEFS` — Play 1/Play 3/Win 1 match), same "no config table, gameplay-adjacent identity lives in code" reasoning as `CARD_POOL`. `quest_progress` (migration `0005`) is one row per `(account, quest, day)`; `recordQuestProgress(pool, accountId, track)` upserts +1 progress (capped at goal) for every quest on that track, `claimQuest` atomically flips `claimed` false→true only when progress already meets the goal (a single `update ... where ... returning` is the whole compare-and-set, no extra lock needed) before crediting Coins.
- **Match hook** (`matchRoom.ts`): right where the existing Coins match-reward fires (the `null → non-null` winner transition), each participant with a real account now also gets `recordQuestProgress(pool, accountId, "play")` and, for the winner, `"win"` — best-effort, same as the Coins award, so a tracking failure can't crash the match.
- **API** (`httpApi.ts`): `GET /api/daily`, `POST /api/daily/claim` (`409` if already claimed today), `GET /api/quests`, `POST /api/quests/:id/claim` (`400` unknown quest, `409` incomplete or already claimed).
- **Tests:** `db.test.ts` covers first-claim streak=1, same-day double-claim rejection, streak extension vs. reset-after-a-gap (simulated by writing `last_daily_claim_day` directly), the 7-day cycle wrapping, quest progress capping at goal per-track, and claim-once-then-reject-a-repeat. `server.test.ts`'s existing real-WS match-to-conclusion test now also polls (`recordQuestProgress` is fire-and-forget, not part of the `matchReward` message, so there's no message to await) for both participants' "play" quest advancing and the winner's "win" quest advancing. `api.test.ts` covers the HTTP-level 409/400s and the no-auth-header 401s. All run against a real local Postgres (Docker `postgres:16-alpine`) this session — 72/72 server tests green.

**New this session (session 9) — Referrals (`referralsRepo.ts`, migration `0006`):** a viral-growth invite loop, added directly at the user's request ("some sort of social invite feature eg free packs or free card to get the viral spread going"). Both sides get a free Standard Pack: the *referred* player's is granted immediately at their first sign-in (they already took the one real action that matters — connect + sign); the *referrer*'s is granted only once the referred account completes its first Play Online match (`matchRoom.ts`'s post-match hook, the same server-validated-event bar Coins/quests already use), so simply creating a wallet doesn't pay out on its own.
- Every account gets a lazily-generated `referral_code` (`accounts.referral_code`, only created on first request — most accounts never share theirs). A `referrals` row (`referrer_id`, `referred_id` unique, `status` pending→rewarded) is created once, at signup, never retroactively for an existing account. `rewardReferrerIfPending`'s `update ... where status = 'pending' returning` is the whole compare-and-set — fires exactly once per referral.
- Free packs reuse the exact same roll/grant/audit-log pipeline as a paid pack open — `packsRepo.ts`'s `openPack` was refactored to extract a shared `rollGrantAndLog(client, accountId, packType, coinsSpent)` so a free grant (`coinsSpent: 0`) is a real, reproducible, auditable `pack_openings` row, not a special case.
- **Known, documented gap:** two wallets under one attacker's control can refer each other and play one match together to farm one free pack each — bounded by the same "needs a live opponent" friction that already caps Play Online Coins farming, not a new hole this feature opens. Not worth a rate limiter for a feature with zero real users yet; flagged in the code, not silently ignored.
- **Client** (`ReferralScreen.tsx`, reachable via "Invite Friends" in the main menu): shows the shareable link + a Copy button, and live Invited/Free-Packs-Earned/Awaiting-First-Match counts. `referral.ts` captures a `?ref=CODE` URL param into `localStorage` at app boot (`main.tsx`) and `useWallet.ts`'s `connect()` sends it (one-shot — consumed regardless of outcome) on the next sign-in.
- **API:** `GET /api/referral` (authenticated — code + stats); `POST /api/auth/verify` gained an optional `referralCode` field, applied only when `Account.isNew` (best-effort — a garbled/unknown code never blocks sign-in).
- **Tests:** `db.test.ts` covers code generation/stability, signup granting a free pack + a pending referral row, unknown-code/self-referral no-ops, the reward flipping exactly once, and a no-op for an unrelated account. `api.test.ts` covers the HTTP-level flow and auth. `server.test.ts` gained a real end-to-end WS test: a referred account plays an actual match to a real conclusion and the referrer's stats flip to rewarded. 88/88 server tests green (real local Postgres). Also verified with a 3-wallet scripted-browser (Playwright) run: a referral link generated and followed by a second real signup, confirmed the referred account received 59 card instances (54 starting Commons + a 5-card free pack) and the referrer's screen updated from 0→1 pending immediately, then to 1 rewarded/0 pending after the reward fired.

# 5. Multiplayer (`server/`)

Matches architecture.md Section 5: FIFO matchmaking, one `MatchState` per room held in server memory, intents validated against the socket that sent them, state broadcast to both players after every legal action, opponent-disconnect notification.

**New this session (session 5) — Coins for playing Play Online:** `findMatch` (`shared/src/index.ts`) gained an optional `token`; `createMatchServer.ts` resolves it via `verifySessionToken` (never throws — a missing/bad/expired token is just anonymous play, exactly as before) and stashes the result as `Session.accountId`. `MatchRoom.handleIntent` (`matchRoom.ts`) captures whether `state.winner` was already decided before applying an intent, and awards Coins exactly once, right at the `null → non-null` transition — `100` for a win, `25` for a loss, `50` for a draw (`coinsRepo.ts`'s `awardMatchResult`/`MATCH_WIN_COINS` etc., placeholder numbers with the same "not tuned economy design" caveat as `WELCOME_BONUS_COINS`), to whichever of the two sessions has a real `accountId` — silently skipped (no crash, no message) for anonymous sessions or when `DATABASE_URL` isn't configured. A new `matchReward` server message carries the amount and new balance back to the client. Play vs AI is deliberately excluded — it's entirely client-side with no server-validated outcome, so awarding Coins there would trust a client-reported win.

Verified with a real WS-level integration test (`server/test/server.test.ts`) that drives an actual match to a genuine engine-decided win over live sockets (a scripted "develop board, respect Guard, attack face" player reacting only to real server-broadcast state — not reaching into `MatchRoom` internals), both anonymous (confirms no `matchReward`, no crash) and authenticated against real Postgres (confirms both accounts get credited, winner's `coinsEarned` > loser's, and the reported balance matches a fresh `getBalance` read) — 46/46 server tests passing. Also verified visually end-to-end: a real scripted-wallet browser client playing a live online match against a raw-WebSocket scripted opponent, confirming the "+25 Coins" banner actually renders in `OnlineMatch.tsx` after a real loss.

**Known limitations, not yet addressed:**
- Skill-based matchmaking — currently just "whoever's next in the queue"
- No reconnect-to-in-progress-match — a dropped connection ends the match for both players
- State lives in one process's memory — fine at current scale, won't horizontally scale past one server instance without more work

# 6. Architecture Decisions Made Since `architecture.md` Was Written

`architecture.md` is the design doc; this section is the "here's what we actually did" reconciliation. (Also reflected in `architecture.md` Section 13 directly.)

- **Client:** web-first (React + Vite), not Unity — confirmed and shipped.
- **Battle engine language:** TypeScript — confirmed and proven in production, including the server running it live via `tsx` (see `server/README.md` for why `tsx` in production rather than a compiled `tsc` build — the workspace packages point `main` at raw `.ts` source, so a plain `node dist/index.js` would crash).
- **Hosting split:** Vercel (client, static) + Railway (match server, needs a persistent WebSocket process — Vercel can't host that). This is the practical resolution of the open question architecture.md Section 13 flagged.
- **Monorepo shape:** `engine` / `shared` (protocol) / `server` / `client` as npm workspaces. `shared` wasn't in the original architecture doc's package list — it exists because `MatchState` has `Set`-typed fields and an `rng` function that don't survive `JSON.stringify`, so both sides needed one place owning that serialization boundary.
- **Identity/auth: wallet-connect from day one, overriding Section 9's "email/social first, wallet later."** Deliberate call, not an oversight — Section 9's reasoning (protect non-crypto-native players from wallet friction at signup) assumed a broad launch audience; this project's actual go-to-market is crypto-native (token launch, Web3-community acquisition — see Section 0), where a wallet is *lower* friction than inventing a password. The one piece of Section 9's intent kept: no login wall anywhere — Play vs AI/Online need no wallet at all, it's asked for only when something needs to persist.
- **Custodial-wallet question (Section 13, "still open"): now moot for the current stage.** Nothing here needs a custodial wallet yet — deck-saving only needs an address to sign in with, no asset custody. Revisit once minting/marketplace (Section 8) are actually being built.
