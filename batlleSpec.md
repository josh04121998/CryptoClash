# CRYPTO CLASH

## Simplified Battle System Specification

### Version 0.2 — Core Combat Rules

---

# 1. Battle Philosophy

The battle system follows one rule above all others:

> **If a player understands the basics after one match, the system is doing its job.**

Depth should come from decisions rather than rules complexity.

The intended experience is:

**Simple controls + understandable cards + deep combinations.**

---

# 2. Match

Two players.

Each starts with:

**30 HP**

Each uses:

**30-card deck**

Each starts with:

**4 cards**

Each has:

**5 battlefield slots**

---

# 3. Turn Loop

Every turn is:

### 1. Gain Energy

Maximum Energy increases by 1, up to 10.

Energy refills.

### 2. Draw

Draw one card.

### 3. Play

Play creatures, spells and items.

### 4. Attack

Attack enemy creatures or the enemy player.

### 5. End

Turn passes to the opponent.

That's the fundamental game.

---

# 4. Energy

Energy is the game's only primary resource.

Turn 1:

**1 Energy**

Turn 2:

**2 Energy**

Turn 3:

**3 Energy**

...

Turn 10:

**10 Energy**

Maximum:

**10**

Unused Energy disappears at the end of the turn.

This makes every turn a resource-management decision.

---

# 5. Battlefield

Each player has five slots:

**[ 1 ] [ 2 ] [ 3 ] [ 4 ] [ 5 ]**

Players choose where to place creatures.

Slots 1 and 5 are **Flanks**.

Slots 2–4 are **Center**.

Positioning matters primarily through card abilities.

Example:

> "Adjacent Doggos gain +1 Attack."

That's enough to make positioning meaningful without creating a complicated lane system.

---

# 6. Creatures

Every creature has:

**Energy Cost**

**Attack**

**Health**

Example:

### Moon Dog

**3 Energy**

**4 Attack**

**4 Health**

> Gain +1 Attack while next to another Doggo.

---

# 7. Summoning

Creatures enter an empty slot.

Creatures normally cannot attack on the same turn they are played.

This prevents players from dumping creatures onto the board and immediately dealing massive damage.

**Rush** bypasses this restriction.

---

# 8. Attacking

A creature can normally attack:

* An enemy creature
* The enemy player

Each creature can normally attack:

**once per turn**

Combat is deliberately straightforward.

---

# 9. Creature Combat

When a creature attacks another creature:

Both deal damage to each other.

Example:

**5/4** attacks **3/6**

Result:

First creature:

**5/4 → 5/1**

Second creature:

**3/6 → 3/1**

Both survive.

---

# 10. Direct Damage

If allowed by the current board state, creatures can attack the enemy player directly.

Direct damage is the primary way to win.

Aggressive decks can therefore attempt to race the opponent.

Defensive decks can build boards to stop them.

---

# 11. Guard

Guard is the game's primary defensive mechanic.

A creature with Guard protects its player.

The opponent must deal with the Guard before freely attacking the player.

Guard should be visually obvious.

Example:

**🛡️ GUARD**

No complicated rules text is necessary.

---

# 12. Rush

Rush means:

> **This creature can attack immediately.**

Example:

**3 Energy — 4/3 — Rush**

The player can summon it and immediately use it to attack.

Rush creates exciting tempo plays without introducing additional complexity.

---

# 13. Stealth

Stealth means:

> **The opponent can't normally target this creature until it reveals itself.**

Stealth normally breaks when the creature attacks.

Stealth creates surprise and tactical timing.

---

# 14. Burn

Burn represents direct or ongoing damage.

Examples:

> Deal 3 damage to the enemy player.

or:

> Deal 1 damage at the end of each turn.

Burn should be visually and mechanically simple.

---

# 15. HODL

HODL is the signature "wait for it" mechanic.

Example:

### Diamond Hands

**4 Energy**

**2/6**

> At the start of your turn, gain +1 Attack.

The longer it survives, the more valuable it becomes.

HODL creates a simple strategic question:

> "Do I protect this or use my resources elsewhere?"

---

# 16. Volatility

Volatility is the game's one major global mechanic.

A shared meter exists:

**VOLATILITY 0 / 10**

Some cards increase it.

Some cards reduce it.

When it reaches 10:

**A Market Event happens.**

Then it resets to 0.

---

# 17. Market Events

Market Events are deliberately simple.

Example:

## 📉 MARKET CRASH

> Deal 2 damage to the strongest creature on each side.

Other possible events:

### 📈 PUMP

All creatures gain +1 Attack this turn.

### 💧 LIQUIDATION

Players lose 2 Energy next turn.

### 🚀 FOMO

Both players draw a card.

### 🦅 BLACK SWAN

A random global effect occurs.

The player does not need to understand a complicated system.

They simply understand:

> **"When Volatility hits 10, something happens."**

---

# 18. Why Volatility Exists

Volatility provides a unique strategic layer without requiring complicated rules.

Players can decide:

> "I'm going to trigger the Market Crash."

Or:

> "I'm going to lower Volatility before my opponent gets there."

Experienced players will learn to build decks around this.

New players can simply ignore it initially.

---

# 19. Faction Mechanics

Each faction should have one simple identity.

### Doggos

**Swarm**

More Doggos = stronger.

### Frogs

**Copy**

Copy or transform cards.

### Degens

**Risk**

Trade HP/resources for power.

### Crypto Bros

**Grow**

Generate resources and scale.

### Builders

**Combo**

Reward playing cards in sequence.

### Normies

**Adapt**

Flexible cards that work in many situations.

---

# 20. Card Complexity Rule

Most cards should communicate their purpose immediately.

Good:

> **Deal 3 damage.**

Good:

> **Adjacent Doggos gain +1 Attack.**

Good:

> **Draw 2 cards.**

Good:

> **Gain +1 Attack each turn.**

Avoid early cards with paragraphs of rules text.

The initial card pool should be deliberately simple.

---

# 21. Strategy Comes From Combinations

Individual cards should be easy to understand.

The strategy emerges when they interact.

Example:

### Doggo Pack Leader

> Adjacent Doggos gain +1 Attack.

*

### Puppy Swarm

> Summon two 1/1 Puppies.

*

### Pack Rush

> Give your Doggos Rush this turn.

Each card is simple.

Together they create a powerful strategy.

---

# 22. Archetypes

The game should naturally create:

### Aggro

Attack quickly.

### Midrange

Efficient creatures and strong board.

### Control

Remove threats and survive.

### Combo

Combine cards for powerful turns.

### Volatility

Manipulate the Market.

### Swarm

Build a wide board.

### Sacrifice

Use creatures as resources.

Players should not need to understand these terms to play.

They emerge naturally.

---

# 23. Randomness

Randomness is allowed.

But randomness should create:

**Excitement, not confusion.**

Good:

> "Choose one of three random cards."

Less desirable:

> "A random effect happens from a table of 47 possible outcomes."

Players should generally understand the range of possible outcomes.

---

# 24. Hand Size

Maximum hand:

**10 cards**

If the player would draw beyond 10, the excess card is discarded.

This prevents unlimited hand accumulation.

---

# 25. Empty Deck

If a player tries to draw from an empty deck, they take increasing fatigue damage.

First failed draw:

**1 damage**

Then:

**2**

Then:

**3**

Then:

**4**

This prevents infinite games.

---

# 26. Match Length

Target:

**7–12 minutes**

Upper target:

**~15 minutes**

The game should feel fast enough that players can immediately queue for another match.

---

# 27. Turn Timer

Initial target:

**60 seconds**

A small time bank can be provided for difficult turns.

Exact timing should be adjusted through testing.

---

# 28. Matchmaking

Players should initially be matched primarily according to skill/rating.

Collection value should not determine matchmaking.

Wallet value should not determine matchmaking.

Staking level should not determine matchmaking.

---

# 29. Competitive Fairness

A player's:

* Wallet
* NFT collection
* Token balance
* Staking amount

should not directly modify their combat stats.

The battle system should remain its own competitive environment.

---

# 30. Server Authority

The server determines:

* Card draws
* Energy
* Damage
* HP
* Card effects
* Random outcomes
* Victory
* Rewards

The client displays the game.

It does not decide the outcome.

---

# 31. Replays

Every match should generate a simple action history.

Example:

> Turn 4
> Player A plays Moon Dog
> Player A attacks
> Moon Dog deals 4 damage
> Player B plays Guard
> Player A increases Volatility
> Volatility reaches 10
> MARKET CRASH
> Market Crash deals 2 damage

This supports:

* Replays
* Spectating
* Anti-cheat
* Debugging
* Competitive review

---

# 32. First Prototype

The first combat prototype only needs:

* 30 HP
* 30-card decks
* 5 slots
* Energy
* Creatures
* Attack
* Health
* Basic spells
* Guard
* Rush
* Basic positioning
* Six faction identities
* Volatility
* Market Events

Everything else can wait.

---

# 33. Battle System North Star

The player should be thinking:

> **"What should I play?"**

> **"Where should I put it?"**

> **"Should I attack?"**

> **"Should I save my Energy?"**

> **"Should I trigger Volatility?"**

> **"Can I set up a combo next turn?"**

Not:

> **"Wait, what does this obscure rule mean?"**

That is the core design philosophy of CRYPTO CLASH.

---

# 34. Final Battle Design Goal

The ideal experience is:

### First Match

**"Oh, I get it."**

### Tenth Match

**"I think I'm getting good at this."**

### Hundredth Match

**"Holy shit, I could have played that turn completely differently."**

### Thousandth Match

**"I understand this game at a completely different level."**

That is the target.
